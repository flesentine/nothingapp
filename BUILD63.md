# Build 63

Creditor identity acquired a market price. Build 62 made sovereign debt negotiable through creditor conferences, collective-action votes, exchange bonds, and holdout litigation. That created claims with sharply different legal positions: some creditors accepted replacement paper, some were dragged into it, some escaped it, and some accelerated old promises after a failed exchange. Build 63 asks the next consequence: if those positions have different expected recoveries, why should the same creditor continue to own them?

Build 63 creates the Interreality Distressed Claims Market above the restructuring office. Build 62 debt claims and exchange bonds can now be listed, marked, purchased, resold, and held by persistent investment funds. The market separates face value from market value. A transfer changes the identity and economic basis of the creditor without changing the debtor's external-debt face amount. The debtor can therefore wake up owing exactly the same amount to a completely different personality.

The market begins with three buyers. Archive Recovery Partners is a litigation-oriented fund that prefers live holdout claims. Clearing Yield Cooperative prefers restructured exchange bonds and exchanged claims. Rift Mutual Credit behaves as a contrarian buyer of deeply discounted paper. Each fund has persistent cash, positions, market value, unrealized gains or losses, and realized profit. If a fund later resells an asset, sale proceeds and realized profit move to the seller before ownership transfers again. Claims are therefore not merely assignable once; they can circulate.

Build 63 marks each asset as a fraction of face value using its Build 62 legal status together with the debtor's foreign reserves, outstanding external debt, arrears, and any active Build 61 recognized lien. Restored holdouts command more than bound holdouts, while exchange bonds normally trade above unresolved claims. A payment injunction can abruptly reduce an exchange bond's mark without changing its principal. The simulation can now contain `same face amount, lower market value` as a first-class state.

A litigation fund that acquires a live holdout can seek an equal-treatment payment injunction. The injunction does not seize sovereign reserves and does not itself increase debt. Instead it places outstanding Build 62 exchange bonds for the same debtor under a payment cloud, reducing their market mark. The important mutation is indirect: a stranger can buy a discounted lawsuit and make securities held by completely different creditors less valuable. Build 63 records that event through trust audits, incidents, memories, places, and a persistent injunction action.

The secondary owner can also make a voluntary settlement demand. A restored holdout normally asks for 88% of face, an accelerated holdout for 82%, and another live holdout for 86%. If the debtor accepts and has enough foreign reserves, the cash payment extinguishes the full claim face, reduces external debt by that face amount, reduces the linked Build 61 surviving-debt award, removes the claim's embedded portion from any outstanding Build 62 exchange bond, releases the associated payment injunction, and realizes the buyer's profit or loss against its purchase basis. Sovereign reserve immunity is not bypassed because payment occurs only through voluntary settlement.

This creates a deliberately strange but coherent bargain. A debtor may pay less than the old promise and still erase the whole claim. The secondary buyer may receive less than face and still make money because it bought the claim even cheaper. Both sides can call the same discount a victory for incompatible reasons. New places include `a legal claim acquired a sticker price`, `the debtor woke up owing the same amount to a different personality`, `a stranger bought the lawsuit and the new bonds became less valuable`, `the old promise reached forward and touched the replacement bond`, and `debtor paid less than face to a stranger who still made money`.

Build 63 persists through `nothing-state-v63`, wraps the existing save/render chain, loads after `restructuring.js`, and extends the forget action through version 63. It reads and selectively updates Build 56 foreign reserves, Build 57 external debt, Build 61 surviving-debt awards, and Build 62 creditor claims, conferences, and exchange bonds. It does not rewrite the historical restructuring vote or the historical identity of prior creditors; original ownership is retained on the asset when the first secondary trade occurs.

The Build 63 harness covers purchase, injunction, settlement, and resale. In the main path, a restored Archive holdout with 2.76 face was marked near 71 cents and purchased by Archive Recovery Partners for about 1.9632. The resulting payment injunction reduced the linked exchange-bond mark by 18 percentage points. An 88%-of-face voluntary settlement then paid 2.4288, extinguished the full 2.76 claim, reduced external debt and the linked Build 61 award from 9.9672 to 7.2072, reduced the exchange bond to 7.2072, released the injunction, and produced about 0.4656 of realized buyer profit. In the resale path, the same purchased claim was relisted and sold from Archive Recovery Partners to Rift Mutual Credit; seller cash was credited, the first fund's position changed to sold, the new fund became owner, and the first fund realized about 0.0563 of trading profit. `claims_market.js` passes Node syntax validation.
