# Build 59

The stabilization institution acquired governance. Build 58 created the Interreality Stabilization Fund as a third balance sheet holding pooled RA and RB reserves, quota shares, RDR drawing rights, conditional programs, reviews, waivers, and debt restructurings. Build 59 makes the quota numbers operational by creating a persistent Interreality Stabilization Fund Board. Reality A and Reality B each receive an executive director whose voting weight is derived from the live stabilization quota system. Major board action requires a 60% weighted majority. The board also has a non-quota staff chair whose emergency powers exist specifically because two formally equal sovereign members can otherwise be unable to act.

The signature governance rule is borrower recusal. When a motion concerns a Reality A stabilization program, the Reality A director is recused; the same applies to Reality B. At the original 50/50 quota split, the remaining creditor director has only about half of total board voting power and therefore cannot reach the 60% major-action threshold alone. A rescue-tranche motion can consequently deadlock even when both the borrower and the fund need a decision. The staff chair can invoke a systemic-necessity override, execute the deadlocked motion, reduce fund independence and credibility, and create the institutional paradox `unelected chair became the missing majority`.

Build 59 gives quota reviews real consequences. Formal quota, prior reserve contributions, remaining foreign-reserve capacity, export activity, RDR balances, and stabilization-program exposure all feed a live voting-weight calculation. A member can subscribe additional quota by transferring more of the foreign reserve asset it actually holds into the Build 58 pool. The reserve contribution raises its quota and can increase its voting share. Once a member crosses 60%, it becomes a dominant creditor majority capable of approving major program decisions for the other reality while that borrower is recused. The simulation can therefore move from equal-sovereign deadlock to creditor dominance without changing the constitutional identity of either reality; foreign-reserve contributions become board jurisdiction.

The board receives persistent surveillance reports on each reality. Surveillance measures the Build 57 current account, foreign-currency external debt, arrears, Build 56 reserve stock, Build 55 monetary credibility, and distress in Build 47 package treasuries. The resulting risk score becomes an input into director voting. Technical staff therefore do not merely describe the borrower. Their report can determine whether a director votes to tighten conditionality, soften it, release another reserve tranche, ratify a failed-review waiver, or suspend the program. This produces places such as `technical staff report becoming a voting instruction` and makes surveillance another route by which observation becomes policy authority.

Board conditionality acts directly on the existing Build 58 program. A passed tightening motion raises the monetary-tightening target used by the next Build 58 program review and also increases domestic package-support taxes, so a creditor-weighted vote can make the next reserve tranche harder to unlock. A softening motion lowers that target. A release-tranche motion directly transfers a finite amount of the program currency from the Build 58 reserve pool into the borrower's Build 56 reserve stock. A suspension motion can halt program access. A waiver already granted by Build 58 can later be politically ratified by the board, preserving a distinction between operational emergency action and subsequent governance legitimacy.

The local Build 59 harness demonstrates both constitutional extremes. In the first test, Reality A was the borrower and therefore recused. Reality B held roughly half the board vote, below the 60% threshold, so a reserve-tranche motion deadlocked. The staff chair then used systemic necessity to override the deadlock and release reserves. In the second test, Reality B repeatedly subscribed additional foreign reserves into the fund; after quota reviews its vote share rose to 68.8%. B could then pass a motion tightening A's stabilization conditions without A voting, raising A's live monetary-tightening requirement from 8% to 11%. The same institution can therefore fail because members are too equal or dominate because one member supplied enough reserve resources to become more equal.

Build 59 emits board creation, quota reviews, passed board decisions, and board tranches into the existing Build 40 trust-audit stream. Fund-governance crises open ordinary observability incidents. Build 59 tracks the stabilization board, executive directors, weighted voting shares, quota reviews, additional quota subscriptions, borrower recusal, 60% major-action threshold, surveillance missions, risk recommendations, program motions, board votes, deadlocks, staff-chair systemic-necessity overrides, dominant-creditor majorities, conditionality tightening and softening, board-authorized reserve tranches, waiver ratification, program suspension, trust-log integration, incidents, and fund-governance crises. Crises can create places such as `equal sovereignty unable to authorize the rescue`, `reserve contribution became a unilateral policy vote`, `foreign creditor vote raised the domestic policy hurdle`, `emergency governance replacing quota consent`, `quota share becoming policy jurisdiction`, and `creditor majority governing a borrower that cannot vote`, leaving the bailiff asking whether reserve contributions are money, votes, or sovereignty.
