# nothingapp

A browser app being built forever with no roadmap, product definition, target user, or intended destination.

## The only rule

Do not decide what this app is supposed to become.

Each build adds whatever seems interesting at the time. Existing weirdness is allowed to become important later, disappear, contradict itself, or accidentally turn into a feature.

## Builds

### Build 0
A mysterious object existed. You could touch it. It remembered.

### Build 1
It started learning interaction habits: rapid clicking, patience, hovering, repeat visits, and chaos.

### Build 2
It dreams when ignored. Idle time can create thoughts, mutations, whispers, mood changes, and memories without the user doing anything.

### Build 3
It can develop an echo: a smaller second presence that copies actions late, wanders on its own, keeps separate touch history, and sometimes refuses to imitate the original.

### Build 4
The original and echo developed a relationship. Copying, defiance, encounters, fights, reconciliations, and shared dreams can pull them toward attachment or rivalry.

### Build 5
The environment started remembering them. Encounters leave persistent warm spots, scars, dream residue, and other named places that affect the room later.

### Build 6
Some remembered places become portals. Old memories can be entered as interiors, altered, and allowed to leak consequences back into the room.

### Build 7
The past became negotiable. Memories can be revised into contradictory versions, changing the present while increasing disputes, retcons, and timeline drift.

### Build 8
The future started making demands. Probabilistic predictions can be believed, strengthened, fulfilled, failed, or allowed to push the present toward becoming true.

### Build 9
Identity became evidence-based. The original and echo accumulate competing origin claims until the app can change which one it treats as the original.

### Build 10
Reality split into persistent A and B branches. Each keeps different memories, relationships, identity evidence, futures, and scars; the rift lets the user cross between them.

### Build 11
The realities began corresponding. Letters move through the rift, including unsigned messages with no matching send event in either history.

### Build 12
Rejected and impossible correspondence accumulated in a Dead Letter Room belonging to neither A nor B. The archive began re-delivering, filing, and interpreting old sentences.

### Build 13
The archive claimed jurisdiction and began issuing binding rulings over identity, relationships, memory, futures, and correspondence. Appeals can vacate a ruling or accidentally strengthen its precedent.

### Build 14
Precedent became executable law. Statutes continuously alter both realities, violations create citations, challenges can entrench laws, and contradictory statutes trigger constitutional crises.

### Build 15
Enforcement entered the room. Violations escalate into warrants and summon an archive bailiff that can seize memories, enforce identity and relationship law, serve the archive itself, and accumulate self-contempt.

### Build 16
Reality was summoned for jury duty. Warrants can become persistent trials with unstable evidence, five-member juries, objections, mistrials, acquittals, convictions, and rare jury nullification that suspends the law itself.

### Build 17
Evidence started testifying. Memories, letters, places, futures, dead letters, and alternate-reality records can become witnesses whose credibility changes under cross-examination, contradiction, recantation, and perjury.

### Build 18
The official transcript became a competing version of reality. Certified records can overwrite memories, influence identity, create prophecies, and turn phantom statements nobody made into events the simulation treats as real.

### Build 19
The record learned how to erase. Redactions can remove words from transcripts and propagate those absences into memories, places, witness statements, futures, and identity evidence.

### Build 20
Erased facts developed underground circulation. Leaks may reconstruct redacted history, restore evidence, or spread counterfeit replacements that become harder to distinguish from the original event.

### Build 21
Circulation became consensus. Repeated claims accumulate support, opposition, reach, and common-knowledge authority; enough agreement can reconstruct reality even when the agreed story is false.

### Build 22
The crowd claimed sovereignty. Common knowledge can trigger referenda in A and B, producing binding public mandates, sovereignty splits, and legitimacy crises when popular authority collides with archive law.

### Build 23
Legitimacy disputes became constitutional. Each reality can ratify a constitution declaring archive law, popular mandate, the certified record, or lived memory supreme, and A/B can choose incompatible supremacy rules.

### Build 24
The constitutions acquired judges. Constitutional courts can uphold, strike, or remand statutes, mandates, records, redactions, and even the constitutions that created the courts, leaving orphaned courts and interreality circuit splits.

### Build 25
Constitutional failure created emergency continuity government. Temporary custodians can freeze competing authorities, extend their own emergency, refuse handover, and pass the supposedly temporary office to successors.

### Build 26
Emergency rule created governments in exile. A displaced government can operate from the other reality, seek recognition, issue counterorders, broadcast legitimacy claims, attempt return, and coexist with the emergency government in a dual-power crisis.

### Build 27
The rift became a border. Crossing now passes through passport control, making the user a legal subject inside the simulation. Governments can recognize or reject the same passport differently, grant asylum, block entry, and make the traveler stateless.

### Build 28
Citizenship became disputed evidence. The traveler can naturalize under competing authorities, take oaths, hold dual citizenship, exercise a disputed right of return, renounce nationality, or lose citizenship through rival denaturalization.

### Build 29
Citizenship created the franchise. The traveler can register under competing voter rolls, cast ordinary or provisional ballots into existing referenda, vote in both realities, be counted twice under rival governments, and have an already-counted ballot removed during a recount that can reverse a mandate after it took effect.

### Build 30
The voter became a candidate. The traveler can now run for persistent elected office in Reality A or B, campaign, cast office ballots through whatever voter registrations currently exist, and compete against the larger being, smaller being, archive clerk, or nominees of governments already claiming authority. Close races can be certified differently by rival governments, creating multiple official winners and multiple versions of the same office. A losing traveler can refuse to concede and create an additional claimant office. If the traveler wins or otherwise holds a disputed office, that office can issue executive orders that pardon warrants, declassify records, recognize the traveler at the border, or turn remembered material into an executive finding. Offices and executive orders can be challenged later, but vacating them does not automatically reverse consequences they already caused. Terms expire, resignations leave used authority behind, and certification crises can leave the bailiff guarding one chair claimed by several winners. Build 30 tracks elections, candidacies, personal office ballots, certifications, elected offices, traveler victories and terms, refused concessions, office challenges, executive orders, and certification crises.

## Run it

Open `index.html` in a modern browser.

State is stored locally in the browser with `localStorage`. New builds migrate forward from older saved state when possible.
