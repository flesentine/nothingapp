# nothingapp

A browser app being built forever with no roadmap, product definition, target user, or intended destination.

## The only rule

Do not decide what this app is supposed to become.

Each build adds whatever seems interesting at the time. Existing weirdness is allowed to become important later, disappear, contradict itself, or accidentally turn into a feature.

## Builds

### Build 0
A mysterious object existed. You could touch it. It remembered.

### Build 1
It started learning interaction habits: rapid clicking, patience, hovering, repeat visits, and chaos.

### Build 2
It dreams when ignored. Idle time can create thoughts, mutations, whispers, mood changes, and memories without the user doing anything.

### Build 3
It can develop an echo: a smaller second presence that copies actions late, wanders on its own, keeps separate touch history, and sometimes refuses to imitate the original.

### Build 4
The original and echo developed a relationship. Copying, defiance, encounters, fights, reconciliations, and shared dreams can pull them toward attachment or rivalry.

### Build 5
The environment started remembering them. Encounters can leave persistent warm spots, scars, dream residue, and other named places in the room. The accumulated history changes the room's mood and can trigger weather. You can also interact with old places and discover what the room remembers.

### Build 6
Some remembered places can become portals. Old warm spots, scars, and dream residue may mature into memory interiors that can be entered. Interacting inside changes the memory, and things can leak back into the main room afterward.

### Build 7
The past became negotiable. Memory interiors can now be revised into different versions of the same event. Warm memories can become scars, scars can become warm memories, dreams can overwrite either, and those retcons feed back into the present relationship. The app tracks disputed memories, timeline drift, revision history, and an increasingly questionable history-confidence score.

### Build 8
The future started making demands. Possible futures appear in the room with probabilities and deadlines. Clicking one means believing it, which raises its probability and lets it nudge the present toward its own fulfillment. Predictions can create reconciliations, fights, shared dreams, portals, distance, or eerie similarity. Failed predictions damage timeline confidence, and sufficiently believed futures can refuse to die and schedule themselves again.

### Build 9
Identity became evidence-based. The original and echo now accumulate competing claims about which one existed first. Touches, dreams, encounters, revised memories, claimed portals, and prophecies can all become origin evidence. If the balance shifts far enough, the app rewrites which entity it treats as the original: their labels, visual prominence, and future predictions change with it. Identity flips themselves damage timeline confidence, so the question of who was first can become permanently unstable.

### Build 10
Reality split. The app now maintains two persistent versions of the room, A and B. Each branch can keep a different relationship, identity verdict, memories, scars, portals, retcons, and possible futures. The unseen branch continues changing while you are elsewhere. A visible rift lets you cross between them, memory interiors can lead into alternate versions, and future predictions can force crossings. As the two realities diverge, they can bleed into one another: places, relationship states, origin evidence, and prophecies may arrive from the other room and become part of the current history.

### Build 11
The realities began corresponding. Notes can travel through the rift from A to B and back again, carrying claims about identity, relationships, lost memories, futures, and the rift itself. You can keep, reject, or answer them, and those choices can alter the room. Some letters arrive signed by nobody with no matching send event in either reality; accepting those unsigned messages can create dream residue and further damage timeline confidence.

### Build 12
Rejected and impossible correspondence stopped disappearing. Dead letters accumulate in a persistent space that belongs to neither Reality A nor Reality B. Once enough undelivered mail exists, the Dead Letter Room becomes enterable. Inside, rejected messages remain as physical records and the archive can begin making decisions of its own: re-delivering old sentences into A or B, filing one letter as evidence in both realities, or turning an undelivered address into a remembered place on both sides. As the archive grows it develops a mood, edits unresolved mail, and can become an active third influence without becoming a third reality.

### Build 13
The Dead Letter Room claimed jurisdiction. Its archive can now issue persistent binding rulings over both Reality A and Reality B. Rulings can decide which being counts as the original, mandate truce or separation, admit rejected sentences into the official historical record, order futures to become nearly inevitable, or declare rejected correspondence admissible evidence anyway. Each ruling accumulates precedent and enforcement history. Rulings can be appealed from the archive or from stamped orders visible in the room, but appeals may be granted, denied, or backfire by strengthening the precedent. Vacating a ruling stops future enforcement without erasing changes it already caused.

### Build 14
Precedent became executable law. Binding rulings can now be codified into persistent statutes that directly alter interaction rules across both realities. The Origin Registration Act continuously biases identity evidence; relationship codes impose mandatory truce or separation; the Evidence Preservation Act can deny memory revisions and preserve old versions; predictive-compliance law boosts prophecy probability and deadlines; and admissibility law can keep rejected correspondence legally operative. Violations create citations. Laws can be challenged, suspended, repealed, or become more deeply entrenched after a failed challenge. Contradictory active laws trigger constitutional crises that damage both timelines because the app is attempting to obey mutually impossible rules.

### Build 15
Enforcement entered the room. Repeated violations can now escalate from citations into persistent warrants aimed at the original, the echo, or eventually the archive itself. Enough violations, active warrants, or constitutional instability dispatches an archive bailiff into the current reality. The bailiff can serve warrants, reinforce the legally recognized identity, enforce relationship bounds, impound remembered places, accelerate court-favored futures, and accumulate its own enforcement history. Warrants can be contested, mercy can be requested, and contradictory laws can put the bailiff into self-contempt until it issues paperwork against the office that created it. Build 15 also split the growing single-file implementation into `index.html`, `style.css`, `core.js`, and `app.js`.

### Build 16
Reality was summoned for jury duty. Warrants can now be stayed for persistent trials before enforcement. Each hearing assembles evidence from the actual simulation state: memories, remembered places, correspondence, prophecies, dead letters, statutes, and records from the other reality. A five-member jury is made from those same categories of unstable evidence. You can submit a memory, submit a letter, object to evidence, or request an early verdict. Conviction authorizes the bailiff to serve the warrant; acquittal dismisses it; mistrial returns the unresolved warrant to circulation; and rare jury nullification suspends the law itself. Unsigned correspondence can contaminate the jury room, and warrants against the archive can now put the institution that created the court on trial.

### Build 17
The evidence started testifying. Evidence already admitted into an active hearing can now become a persistent witness with its own credibility, side, examination history, and statement. Memories, letters, places, futures, dead letters, and records from the other reality can take the stand. Cross-examination can strengthen testimony, expose contradictions, cause recantation, or reveal perjury that changes which side the evidence supports and damages timeline confidence. A witness can be confronted with the other reality, placed into witness protection at an address that officially does not exist, or quietly change its wording while nobody is questioning it. Because witnesses mutate the same evidence objects the jury later weighs, testimony can materially change verdicts rather than simply decorating the courtroom.

### Build 18
The official transcript became a competing version of reality. Every trial now develops a persistent written record assembled from court notes, evidence, witness statements, and verdicts. Transcripts can be opened, corrected, and certified. Once certified, the record is no longer passive: its wording is pushed back into both realities as official memory, can overwrite later witness statements, bias identity evidence, and turn recorded future-tense claims into high-probability prophecies. Correction requests can succeed or be denied; a denied correction makes the disputed wording more authoritative. Court reporters can also create phantom lines that nobody remembers saying. If a phantom line enters a certified transcript, both realities begin treating it as an event that happened, creating timeline drift, disputes, and remembered places for an official event with no underlying occurrence. The app now tracks certified transcripts, corrections, phantom lines, and record divergence between what happened and what the paperwork says happened.

### Build 19
The record learned how to erase. Transcript lines can now be redacted, and black bars are causally active rather than merely visual. Redacting a term can mask matching memories in both realities, rename remembered places to `[REDACTED]`, censor witness statements, weaken pending prophecies, and destabilize identity evidence when words such as original or echo disappear from the official record. Redactions on certified transcripts are sealed with higher authority and can continue propagating their absence. You can file a motion to unseal a redaction; a successful motion restores the wording to the transcript but cannot guarantee that erased memories return, while a failed motion strengthens the redaction and increases record drift. Certified records can also redact themselves autonomously, so secrecy can slowly become nonexistence without any user action. The app tracks active and sealed redactions, motions, erased facts, and redaction-driven timeline drift.

### Build 20
Erased facts developed an underground circulation. Active and sealed redactions can now spawn persistent unauthorized leaks sourced from the other reality, dead letters, protected witnesses, anonymous clerks, or nobody at all. Every leak has its own credibility and may be genuine or counterfeit. You can publish a leak, try to verify it against the other reality, or suppress it. Publishing a genuine leak can partially reconstruct transcript wording, restore redacted place names and witness statements, rebuild censored futures, and inject the disclosure directly into an active trial as unauthorized evidence. Counterfeit leaks instead create replacement memories and additional timeline drift, and in some cases the other reality can mistakenly corroborate the counterfeit, allowing a false reconstruction to become “verified.” Suppression strengthens the underlying redaction but can trigger a Streisand effect that creates more copies. As leak heat rises, autonomous document dumps can publish several circulating leaks at once across both realities. The app tracks circulating leaks, publications, verification, false leaks, restored fragments, suppression, document dumps, and leak heat.

### Build 21
Circulation became consensus. Published and verified leaks now produce persistent public claims with support, opposition, reach, and a belief percentage. Claims spread autonomously, and repetition can begin counting as corroboration as reach and momentum grow. You can repeat a claim, challenge it, or poll both realities; all three actions increase its visibility, so even opposition can accidentally help a story become common knowledge. Once a claim reaches enough people with sufficient support, it becomes causally authoritative: genuine consensus can restore redacted places and testimony, rewrite the linked certified transcript as COMMON KNOWLEDGE, influence identity evidence, and create high-probability futures from widely accepted predictions. Counterfeit claims can also win consensus, producing false shared memories and timeline damage simply because enough copies agree. If incompatible claims tied to the same redaction both become common knowledge, the app records a consensus fracture and allows Reality A and Reality B to adopt different obvious truths from the same missing source. The app tracks common knowledge, false consensus, challenges, polls, echo-chamber boosts, restored fragments, and consensus fractures.

### Build 22
The crowd claimed sovereignty. Common-knowledge claims can now become persistent referenda, with Reality A and Reality B voting separately rather than being averaged into one electorate. Campaigning for or against a proposal raises its visibility either way, and ballots can decide whether to declassify a redaction, pardon a warrant, reject an archive statute, recognize one being as the original, or elevate a public claim into a binding mandate. If both realities approve, the resulting mandate can act across both branches; if only one approves, a sovereignty split is recorded and the two realities can operate under different public authority. Public mandates keep enforcing themselves after the vote and can directly conflict with active archive laws, sealed records, or warrants. Those collisions create legitimacy crises, damage timeline confidence, leave remembered places called `two governments`, and can reduce the bailiff to waiting for somebody to establish which sovereign it is supposed to obey. Mandates can be challenged, but a failed challenge increases their authority and public support. The app tracks referenda, turnout, passed and failed votes, binding mandates, sovereignty splits, mandate challenges, and legitimacy crises.

### Build 23
Legitimacy disputes became constitutional. Reality A and Reality B can now convene separate constitutional conventions and draft persistent constitutions that declare one source of authority supreme: archive law, the electorate and its mandates, the certified record, or lived memory inside that reality. Drafts can be campaigned for or against and ratified by their own reality. Once ratified, the supremacy clause actively strengthens the chosen authority: popular constitutions reinforce mandates, archive constitutions entrench statutes, record constitutions increase the authority of certified transcripts and sealed redactions, and memory constitutions can resist record drift and sometimes restore things paperwork erased. Ratified constitutions can be amended, and failed amendments make the existing charter more entrenched. If Reality A and Reality B ratify different supremacy clauses, the app records a constitutional partition, damages both timelines, and leaves remembered places called `supremacy border`. A constitution can also collide with the authority it demotes, producing supremacy crises and places called `supremacy dispute`. The bailiff can now describe itself as obeying a reality's constitution even while older laws and public mandates continue issuing incompatible commands.

### Build 24
The constitutions acquired judges. Each reality with a ratified constitution can now found its own persistent constitutional court and petition for judicial review of active statutes, public mandates, certified transcripts, sealed redactions, or the constitution itself. Cases accept arguments from both sides before a five-vote decision to uphold, strike, or remand the challenged authority. Upholding something strengthens it: statutes become more entrenched, mandates gain authority, certified records become harder to contradict, redactions become harder to unseal, and constitutions become more legitimate. Striking something has real consequences: laws are suspended, mandates repealed, transcripts vacated, and redactions overturned. A court can also invalidate the constitution that created it, leaving behind an orphaned high court that has lost its own legal foundation but continues to exist and issue opinions. Rehearing petitions can reopen precedent or, when denied, make a decision harder to dislodge. If Reality A and Reality B review the same authority and reach opposite holdings, the app records an interreality circuit split, damages both histories, creates remembered places called `split precedent`, and leaves the bailiff trying to follow incompatible supreme-court opinions. The app tracks courts, constitutional cases, opinions, dissents, struck and upheld authorities, remands, judicial precedent, circuit splits, and constitutional self-invalidations.

## Run it

Open `index.html` in a modern browser.

State is stored locally in the browser with `localStorage`. New builds migrate forward from older saved state when possible.
