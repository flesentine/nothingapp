# Build 57

Foreign exchange acquired a balance of payments. Build 56 gave Reality A and Reality B separate settlement currencies, foreign-reserve stocks, pegs, speculative attacks, and a reserve-currency hierarchy; Build 57 gives those currencies something concrete to pay for across the rift. Critical package namespaces can now export ongoing software support, compatibility work, and update access to the other reality under persistent trade contracts. Each contract has an exporter, importer, package namespace, invoice currency, recurring price, criticality, invoice schedule, arrears state, and support status. Reality A and Reality B each maintain external accounts tracking exports, imports, current-account balance, foreign-currency debt, arrears, and cumulative reserve drain.

The central constraint is invoice currency. By default, cross-reality software support is invoiced in the seller's settlement currency. An importer therefore has to spend the Build 56 foreign reserves held by its monetary authority to pay for the service. Domestic money creation cannot directly settle an invoice denominated in the other reality's money. A reality can be fully capable of issuing its own settlement reserves and still fail to pay a foreign software supplier because its stock of the required foreign currency has been exhausted. Successful payments move value into the exporting package treasury, convert external demand into Build 47 maintenance funding, and widen the importing reality's current-account deficit.

Unpaid invoices can become supplier credit instead of immediately terminating the relationship. Supplier credit creates persistent external debt denominated in the invoice currency, with interest, service dates, arrears, rollover, and default. This creates a currency-mismatch problem: the number of foreign units owed can stay constant while a Build 56 devaluation or depreciation makes the debt much larger when measured in the debtor's domestic settlement money. Debt service consumes the same finite foreign-reserve stock needed for imports and peg defense. Repeated missed service can default the obligation even when the debtor remains capable of creating unlimited domestic settlement reserves.

External default has operational consequences but does not rewrite deployed history. A default or prolonged invoice arrears can suspend the imported package-support contract. Existing Build 44 artifacts, Build 45 SBOMs, and Build 43 executed state remain intact, but future support and updates stop. If the importing reality has a corresponding local package namespace, maintainers become more fatigued when the foreign compatibility/support feed disappears. A vulnerable package can therefore continue running after the external contract needed to support it has been suspended, creating places such as `package updates stopped at the balance-of-payments border`, `existing binary outlived its foreign support contract`, and `dependency contract defaulted while deployed code kept running`.

Build 57 also lets invoice-currency choice become monetary power. If Build 56 has established RA or RB as the interreality reserve currency, package contracts can switch to reserve-currency invoicing. When the reserve currency is the buyer's domestic money, the buyer can settle without draining foreign reserves while the exporter accumulates the buyer's currency as an external asset. The reserve currency therefore becomes the language of software trade and can reinforce its own international role. Exporters can alternatively agree to invoice in the buyer's currency, shifting exchange-rate risk from the importer to the seller.

A reality facing persistent reserve drain can impose import compression. Lower-priority software-support contracts are suspended to preserve scarce foreign currency, turning balance-of-payments adjustment into dependency austerity. That may protect the reserve stock while increasing local maintenance burden and leaving existing binaries without external support. The simulation can therefore contain a current-account adjustment that is financially stabilizing and operationally destabilizing at the same time.

Build 57 emits import contracts and supplier-credit arrangements into the existing Build 40 trust-audit stream. External defaults and balance-of-payments crises open ordinary incidents for the observability and operations stack. Build 57 tracks external accounts, software export/import contracts, recurring foreign invoices, invoice-currency choice, reserve-currency invoicing, foreign-reserve payments, reserve drain, current-account balances, supplier credit, foreign-currency external debt, debt-service schedules, exchange-rate revaluation of domestic debt burden, debt rollover, arrears, external default, import suspension, import compression, local-maintainer stress, trust-log integration, incidents, and balance-of-payments crises. Crises can create places such as `solvent in domestic money bankrupt in invoice currency`, `domestic printing press unable to print foreign debt service`, `dependency austerity imposed by foreign-reserve shortage`, and `package updates waiting at the border of the balance sheet`, leaving the bailiff asking why a reality that can print money cannot pay its software supplier.
