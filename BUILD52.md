# Build 52

Software liability acquired credit. Reality A and Reality B now each maintain software-credit-rating agencies that rate both Build 47 package treasuries and Build 51 insurers. Ratings are derived from the financial and operational state already created by earlier builds: package cash and maintenance debt, dependency usage, strikes, bankruptcy, governance splits, insurer capital and leverage, claim history, captive ownership, systemic policy concentration, and public guarantees. Ratings range from AAA through D, carry outlooks and model versions, and are published into the existing Build 40 trust-audit stream so Build 41 transparency and later finality can treat credit opinions as institutional history.

Ratings are not passive labels. An insurer that falls below investment grade becomes rating-restricted: existing policies can remain active but receive rating-watch premium pressure, while ordinary Build 51 underwriting stops because the insurer is no longer in the `solvent` pool used for new policies. A D-rated insurer becomes insolvent and its policies can be orphaned. Package treasuries and insurers can issue software-capital obligations: dependency-revenue bonds for package treasuries and surplus notes for insurers. Borrowing immediately supplies cash or capital, but adds leverage, debt service, rating covenants, and exposure to later downgrades.

Every software bond carries a minimum BBB covenant. If a later rating falls below that threshold, bondholders can issue a collateral call against the downgraded institution. Meeting the call drains the package treasury or insurer capital; failing it can default the bond, bankrupt the package treasury, make an insurer insolvent, orphan insurance policies, and open an ordinary SEV1 incident. This makes ratings capable of becoming self-fulfilling: the model predicts deteriorating credit, the downgrade demands scarce cash, the cash demand worsens the issuer, and the resulting default appears to validate the original model.

Package treasuries can respond to bondholder pressure by raising Build 47 dependency taxes. Because those taxes already have a tax-revolt path, servicing software debt can push downstream users to fork the package and split its Build 46 namespace, which can further damage the treasury's rating. Insurer downgrades likewise feed backward into Build 51 by repricing existing policies and restricting new underwriting. Publicly guaranteed insurers can receive a rating benefit even when their standalone condition is weak, creating the possibility of a highly rated institution whose strength derives from the expectation that Reality A or B will rescue it.

The rating agencies themselves can be captured. Issuers may pay for reconsideration or shop multiple agencies and designate the most favorable result. Issuer-paid reviews reduce agency independence and credibility; repeated dependence on one issuer's fees can create a captured ratings agency. Separate agencies can also issue materially different grades for the same infrastructure, producing split ratings such as AA at one agency and B at another. The market can therefore contain multiple formally published definitions of the same creditworthiness while the issuer selects which one to foreground.

Build 52 tracks rating agencies, model versions, independence and credibility, issuer-paid ratings, rating shopping, designated ratings, upgrades, downgrades, split ratings, agency capture, package and insurer credit scores, dependency-revenue bonds, insurer surplus notes, coupon service, collateral, margin calls, bondholder tax pressure, defaults, rating-restricted insurers, policy rating watch, SEV1 capital incidents, trust-log integration, and self-fulfilling downgrade crises. Crises can create places such as `downgrade demanding cash from the downgraded institution`, `rating agency caused the default it predicted`, `dependency tax pledged to bondholders`, `insurance capital funded by borrowed confidence`, `two ratings one treasury`, and `credit rating acting like infrastructure law`, leaving the bailiff asking whether the package is risky because the rating fell or rated risky because everyone obeyed the rating.
