# Build 54

Software derivatives acquired clearing and settlement. Build 53 allowed one software-credit event to create many swaps, synthetic pools, tranches, and collateral chains; Build 54 inserts a persistent central counterparty into each reality so those contracts can be novated, margined, netted, and settled through a common institution. Reality A and Reality B each bootstrap a Software Clearing House with capital, liquidity, a mutualized default fund, guarantee resources, settlement-finality state, members derived from Build 53 derivatives dealers, and a persistent record of defaults and assessments.

Active Build 53 credit-default swaps can be novated into central clearing. Novation preserves the underlying swap and bond reference but adds a cleared position whose member posts initial margin and later variation margin as the referenced Build 52 bond deteriorates. Portfolio netting recognizes when the same derivatives dealer is on opposite sides of cleared swaps and can materially reduce margin while the member is healthy. If the member defaults, close-out netting breaks and the position snaps back to its gross initial-margin basis, producing the deliberately dangerous transition from a small-looking net exposure to a much larger gross exposure at exactly the moment the member is least able to fund it.

Variation-margin calls drain Build 53 dealer capital and collateral. A failed call defaults the clearing member and starts the clearinghouse default waterfall. Settlement first consumes the defaulting member's posted variation and initial margin, then the clearinghouse default fund, then assessments on surviving members. Those assessments can weaken other derivatives dealers and create second-order defaults. Central clearing therefore removes many bilateral counterparty relationships by concentrating them into one institution whose own solvency becomes systemically important.

Build 54 distinguishes trade execution from settlement finality. When a cleared Build 53 swap settles, fails, or enters counterparty default, the obligation is placed into a persistent settlement queue. A settlement cycle can make all fully funded obligations final, or it can fail with a shortfall and freeze the clearinghouse. A failed clearinghouse freezes otherwise-valid cleared contracts and queued settlements without erasing the underlying swaps, bonds, ratings, or package failures that created them. Reality can later supply a public liquidity bridge to reopen the clearinghouse, creating a natural precursor to a lender-of-last-resort institution.

An emergency path can declare the already-failed settlement cycle final even while its original shortfall remains. Settlement items are then marked `final-with-shortfall` rather than being silently repaired. This produces a financial analogue of earlier consensus failures: the institutional record says settlement is final while one or more obligations remain economically unpaid. Places such as `final settlement containing an unpaid obligation`, `payment finality declared over a shortfall`, and `clearinghouse frozen with valid trades waiting inside` preserve the distinction between procedural finality and material completion.

Build 54 emits clearinghouse creation, novation, settlement finality, and liquidity bridges into the existing Build 40 trust-audit stream. Clearing-member and clearinghouse failures also open ordinary incidents, so the Build 37–39 observability and operations stack can respond to settlement infrastructure failure. Build 54 tracks clearinghouses, members, cleared positions, gross and net exposures, initial and variation margin, portfolio-margin benefit, close-out netting breaks, margin calls, member defaults, default-fund use, surviving-member assessments, settlement queues, settlement cycles, ordinary and emergency finality, public liquidity bridges, clearinghouse freezes and failures, trust-log integration, incidents, and clearing crises. Crises can create places such as `institution built to remove counterparty risk became the counterparty`, `net exposure becoming gross exposure at default`, `settlement finality depending on one institution staying solvent`, and `too central to settle and too connected to fail`, leaving the bailiff asking whether a trade exists before or after the clearinghouse says it settled.
