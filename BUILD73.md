# Build 73

Public insolvency acquired a receivership. Build 72 could identify a Build 63 fund whose cash plus junior asset value was insufficient to cover a live Build 71 public recoupment claim, but it deliberately stopped at the flag `publicRecoupmentDistress72='insolvent'`. Build 73 turns that observation into an actual fund-resolution process through the Interreality Fund Resolution Court.

A publicly insolvent Build 63 fund can now enter a persistent `FRCV#` receivership. Commencement sequesters the fund's current cash into estate cash, freezes the fund as a trading counterparty, blocks open Build 63 listings on its assets, and stays the Build 71 public recoupment claim together with any Build 72 public liens. The public creditor therefore cannot keep levying individual assets after the collective case begins.

The stay is deliberately asymmetric. Existing Build 64 repo claims, active Build 65 rehypothecation interests, and open Build 65 title disputes remain outside the estate under safe-harbor treatment. Build 73 records them as resolution claims for visibility, but it does not seize or refinance their collateral. Senior title and rehypothecation claims are priority 1, repo is priority 2, and the stayed public recoupment claim is priority 3. Those priority numbers describe the resolution schedule; asset-specific Build 64/65 mechanics remain authoritative.

Clean positions are moved into a `receivership-held73` state so the old Build 64 origination path can no longer finance them while the case is open. Title-frozen and already-repoed positions keep the older statuses their own layers need. When those senior interests later disappear, Build 73 can bring the newly clean asset into the estate without rewriting the history of how the senior claim ended.

Estate liquidation uses the same late-stack distressed valuation inputs as Builds 64 and 72, but applies a deeper receivership discount. A clean asset is offered at 64% of its current marked value. Another Build 63 fund must have enough cash, cannot itself be in an active receivership, cannot already be a Build 72 publicly insolvent fund, and cannot be a previously liquidated-deficiency fund. A successful sale transfers the real Build 62 claim or bond ownership to that buyer and puts the cash in the estate rather than back into the failed fund's trading balance.

Build 72 liens do not disappear when receivership starts. They become `stayed-receivership73`; if their collateral is sold by the receiver, they become `attached-estate-proceeds73`. Payment still occurs through the underlying Build 71 public claim rather than through a new shadow public-credit ledger.

Estate distributions retire the real public balance sheet. Cash paid to a Build 71 recoupment claim reduces the exact linked Build 55 facility principal, the monetary authority's monetary base and outstanding credit, and Build 55 reserve issuance, and creates an ordinary Build 71 `PRP#` payment record marked as sourced from Build 73. Accrued Build 55 interest survives if principal is fully recouped but old interest remains.

If the public claim is paid in full, the receivership closes as `resolved-public-paid`. Unsold clean assets are restored to normal ownership, any remaining estate cash returns as residual equity cash to the fund, public liens are released, and the Build 72 distress flag becomes recoverable again.

Repo and title safe harbor can instead leave the case stalled. If every remaining asset is still subject to active repo, rehypothecation, or title dispute, the case becomes `stalled-senior-assets`. The public creditor remains stayed while the older private layers resolve themselves. If those assets later leave the fund through the Build 64/65 machinery and residual cash returns to the old fund, Build 73 captures that later cash into the estate before the fund can resume trading.

A clean estate can also fail for lack of buyers. If a saleable asset exists but no eligible Build 63 fund has enough cash at the 64% receivership price, the case becomes `illiquid-estate` rather than inventing a sale or treating the asset as worthless. A later buyer can make the case active again.

If all clean assets are sold, no senior asset remains, estate cash is exhausted, and public recoupment is still unpaid, the fund closes as `liquidated-deficiency`. The Build 71 claim becomes `receivership-deficiency73`; the linked Build 55 facility principal and monetary base remain outstanding at the unpaid amount. Build 73 therefore allows the investment fund to disappear while the public rescue asset survives on the monetary authority's balance sheet.

Build 73 also protects the accounting guard introduced in Build 71. If estate cash exists but the linked Build 55 facility or monetary-authority record is missing, the case becomes `blocked-public-record` rather than moving cash or declaring a false deficiency. A restored valid public record can reactivate the case.

The deterministic clean-resolution harness used the same 2.0 exchange bond from Build 72 with a 0.52 public claim. The current mark was 1.5018181818. Receivership sold the bond for 0.9611636364, or 64% of mark. Exactly 0.52 retired the public claim, Build 55 facility principal and monetary base fell to zero, the buyer became the actual bond owner, and the remaining 0.4411636364 returned to the old fund as estate residual when the case closed `resolved-public-paid`.

The repo safe-harbor harness put a 1.0 active Build 64 repo on the same asset. The case moved to `stalled-senior-assets`. The repo appeared as priority 2 `outside-estate-safe-harbor`; the public claim appeared as priority 3 `stayed-public`; no receivership sale occurred and the 0.52 public facility remained outstanding. After the repo later disappeared and 0.20 cash returned to the old fund, Build 73 captured and distributed that 0.20, then closed the exhausted estate with a 0.32 public deficiency.

The title harness put a 1.20 Build 65 title dispute on the asset. The resolution schedule recorded a priority 1 senior-title claim and the case stalled without selling the paper. The no-bid harness left the clean asset in `receivership-held73`, recorded one failed estate bid, and moved the case to `illiquid-estate`. A tiny-asset harness sold the remaining paper for 0.0480581818 and closed with a 0.4719418182 public deficiency still present in the Build 55 facility and monetary base.

The committed guard harness also verified that a missing monetary-authority record leaves 0.1480581818 of estate cash parked in `blocked-public-record` with no public collection, and that a fund already marked `liquidated-deficiency` cannot become the buyer of another receivership estate merely because it later acquires cash.

Build 73 persists through `nothing-state-v73`, wraps the existing save/render chain, loads after `public_liens.js`, and extends forget-through-v73. It reads and selectively updates Build 55 public credit, Build 61/57/56 valuation inputs, Build 62 claims and bonds, Build 63 fund cash/positions/ownership, Build 64 repo state, Build 65 title/reuse state, Build 71 recoupment claims/payments, and Build 72 liens/distress flags. Historical Build 70 rescue and Build 69 clearing records remain intact.

New places include `bankruptcy stay protecting the bailout collector from itself`, `repo lender walked out with collateral while the public creditor waited in court`, `public lien stopped at the courthouse while repo safe harbor stayed open`, `investment fund became an estate because the bailout bill was larger than what remained`, and `distressed paper sold by a receiver while the old fund stopped trading`. `fund_resolution.js` passes V8 syntax validation. The instrumented harness executes the exact committed Build 73 functions and covers clean resolution, automatic stay, repo safe harbor, title priority, later estate cash capture, no-bid illiquidity, public deficiency, orphaned-public-record blocking, and buyer eligibility guards.
