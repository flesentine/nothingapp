# Build 58

Balance-of-payments crises acquired a stabilization institution. Build 57 made critical package support an external service that can drain foreign reserves, create foreign-currency supplier debt, and default even while a reality remains fully able to issue its own domestic settlement money. Build 58 responds by creating a persistent Interreality Stabilization Fund (ISF), funded by quota contributions from Reality A and Reality B and holding pooled RA and RB reserve assets outside either domestic monetary authority. The ISF is a third balance sheet: it cannot manufacture the other reality's currency at will, but it can pool scarce foreign reserves, allocate reserve drawing rights, lend those reserves in tranches, restructure external software debt, and attach conditions to the rescue.

The fund bootstraps by taking finite quota contributions from the Build 56 foreign-reserve stocks of A and B. Reality A contributes RB and Reality B contributes RA because each is contributing the foreign asset it actually holds. Members also receive RDR (Rift Drawing Rights), a synthetic reserve claim on the pooled institution. RDR can be allocated by the fund and later exchanged for the foreign reserve currency a member needs, but conversion drains the finite RA or RB pool. Drawing rights therefore create a claim on international liquidity without pretending the stabilization institution can print either member's settlement money.

A Reality A or B balance-of-payments program is sized from the Build 57 external position: current-account deficit, invoice arrears, foreign-currency external debt, and the remaining Build 56 reserve stock. The program commits a finite amount of the foreign currency the borrower needs, disburses an initial tranche, and withholds the rest behind periodic reviews. Program conditions reach directly into earlier systems: import compression can suspend lower-priority Build 57 support contracts; monetary tightening raises the Build 55 lender-of-last-resort discount rate; exchange-rate flexibility can force a Build 56 peg to float; and package-revenue reform raises Build 47 dependency-support taxes so more maintenance is financed domestically. The rescue therefore does not merely provide foreign money. It becomes a route by which the external lender can alter domestic package, monetary, trade, and exchange-rate policy.

Program reviews distinguish approval from continuing access. Conditions are checked against the live state. A failed review marks the program off-track and withholds the next reserve tranche even if existing software-support contracts or debt-service schedules are already relying on it. A failed review can be explicitly waived, releasing the tranche anyway while reducing the fund's independence and credibility. Build 58 can therefore contain both `foreign reserves arriving with software policy attached` and `conditionality suspended because withholding reserves became too dangerous`. The rescue institution can become trapped between enforcing its own rules and destabilizing the system those rules were intended to stabilize.

Build 58 also adds external-debt restructuring. Active, arrears, or defaulted Build 57 supplier-credit obligations can receive a principal haircut, partial accrued-interest reduction, lower rate, and extended service schedule. The debtor's external burden falls and a support contract suspended specifically for external-debt default can resume, but the creditor reality absorbs the corresponding loss. The old default is not erased; restructuring creates a new historical fact layered on top of it. This makes external adjustment distributive rather than magical: restoring the debtor's software-support channel means somebody on the exporting side loses a claim.

RDR allocations provide another path to liquidity. The ISF can create new drawing-right claims for A and B, but exchanging RDR for RA or RB consumes the fund's actual pooled reserve asset. A sufficiently large rescue program or repeated drawing-right conversions can therefore exhaust the stabilization institution itself. A funding-shortfall program is possible: the lender can promise support but discover that its reserve pool is finite. Build 58 thus places one more institution above Build 57 without escaping the underlying scarcity of foreign settlement assets.

The stabilization fund emits creation, programs, restructurings, and drawing-right allocations into the existing Build 40 trust-audit stream. Failed reviews and stabilization crises open ordinary incidents for the observability and operations stack. Build 58 tracks the pooled stabilization fund, member quotas and votes, RDR balances and allocations, foreign-reserve pools, stabilization programs, tranche commitments and disbursements, conditionality, periodic reviews, failed reviews, waivers, policy implementation across Builds 47/55/56/57, external-debt restructuring, creditor losses, funding shortfalls, trust-log integration, incidents, and stabilization crises. Crises can create places such as `foreign reserves arriving with software policy attached`, `balance-of-payments lender sitting inside package governance`, `software support waiting for a macroeconomic review`, `dependency austerity written into rescue terms`, `supplier balance sheet absorbed the rescue haircut`, and `conditionality suspended because withholding reserves became too dangerous`, leaving the bailiff asking whether the rescue loan is financing policy or buying it.
