# Build 64

Market price became collateral value, and collateral value became leverage. Build 63 made restructuring claims and exchange bonds tradeable at prices that could diverge sharply from face value. Build 64 adds an Interreality Collateral and Repo Facility above that market and asks the next question: once a distressed claim has a price, why should its owner wait for settlement before spending against it?

A Build 63 investment fund can now pledge an owned debt claim or exchange bond into a repo loan. The lender is the debtor reality's existing Build 54 Software Clearing House when one is available, so the same liquidity pool that previously supported derivative settlement can now finance positions in distressed sovereign paper. If no clearing house is available, the Build 64 facility supplies a smaller fallback liquidity pool. Opening a repo transfers cash to the investment fund without transferring the underlying claim. The asset remains owned by the fund but becomes encumbered and cannot remain openly listed for sale while the repo is active.

Repo capacity is mark-to-market. Build 64 uses the same underlying economic facts that drive the Build 63 market — legal status, foreign reserves, external debt, arrears, active Build 61 liens, and Build 63 payment injunctions — and then applies secured-lending haircuts. Exchange bonds normally receive a 25% haircut, while live holdouts receive materially larger haircuts. A payment injunction both lowers an exchange bond's mark and raises its repo haircut to 45%, creating a deliberately procyclical double hit: the collateral is simultaneously declared worth less and permitted to support less borrowing.

Every active repo is continuously reconciled against its current maximum advance. If the outstanding loan exceeds that amount, the facility issues a persistent margin call. The borrowing fund can meet the call from cash, reducing the repo principal and returning liquidity to the lender. If the market recovers before payment, the call can cure itself. If the call remains unmet, or the borrower voluntarily surrenders the position, the collateral is liquidated.

Forced liquidation prefers another Build 63 fund with enough cash, with Rift Mutual Credit acting as the natural contrarian buyer when possible. The asset changes owners and the sale proceeds repay the secured lender. Any excess belongs to the borrower; any shortfall first consumes the lender's default fund and can then reduce clearing-house capital. A fire sale also creates an eight-point secured-lending discount against other collateral of the same debtor. The sale therefore becomes new evidence used by lenders to mark similar collateral lower, allowing one fund's leverage problem to create another fund's margin problem.

This makes the old Build 54 clearing layer vulnerable to a market that did not exist when the clearing house was created. A clearing house can lose liquidity financing a Build 63 exchange bond, call margin because a Build 63 litigation injunction lowered the bond's value, liquidate the bond into another fund, and spend its default fund covering the difference. The project can now contain `derivatives clearing money was spent rescuing a loan against a lawsuit` without pretending the derivative and sovereign-debt systems were designed together.

Build 64 also handles collateral proceeds rather than allowing a financed asset to disappear behind the lender's back. If a pledged Build 63 holdout is voluntarily settled, the repo layer detects the extinguished claim during the same wrapped render cycle, identifies the settlement cash, and sweeps the secured lender's principal out of those proceeds before the fund can treat the cash as unencumbered. If the settlement proceeds fully repay the repo, the collateral lien is released as `repaid-from-collateral-proceeds`; otherwise the remaining deficiency enters liquidation/default handling. Existing Build 63 listings created against newly pledged collateral are automatically marked `blocked-collateral` before they can be sold.

Build 64 persists through `nothing-state-v64`, wraps the existing save/render chain, loads after `claims_market.js`, and extends the forget action through version 64. It reads and selectively updates Build 54 clearing-house liquidity/default funds, Build 56 foreign reserves, Build 57 external debt and arrears, Build 61 liens, Build 62 claims and exchange bonds, and Build 63 fund ownership/listings/actions. Historical trades, restructuring votes, legal judgments, and settlement records remain intact.

The Build 64 harness covers leverage, margin, liquidation, default-fund contagion, collateral settlement, original-lender continuity, and sale blocking. In the primary path, a 9.36 exchange bond with a Build 61 lien supported a 5.1295 repo advance from the Reality A clearing house. A Build 63 payment injunction reduced the bond mark from about 81.19 cents to 63.19 cents and raised its haircut from 25% to 45%, cutting permissible borrowing to 3.2529 and creating a 1.8765 margin call. After the borrowing fund had spent its available cash, forced liquidation sold the bond to Rift Mutual Credit for about 4.8498, created a 0.2796 shortfall, and consumed exactly 0.2796 of the Build 54 default fund. The fire sale also applied an eight-point collateral discount to the debtor's remaining financed paper. In the proceeds path, a financed 2.76 restored holdout carried a 0.8633 repo; when that claim settled, the settlement cash automatically repaid the repo and the loan closed as `repaid-from-collateral-proceeds`. A separate edge harness confirmed that repayment still returns cash to the original clearing-house lender even if its status changes after origination, and that an attempted Build 63 listing of pledged collateral is automatically changed to `blocked-collateral`. `repo_finance.js` passes Node syntax validation.
