# Build 66

Ownership risk became insurable, but insurance did not make ownership clean. Build 65 created collateral-title chains where a fund could economically own a distressed claim, a Build 64 lender could hold the first repo lien, a downstream lender could hold a senior rehypothecation lien, and a fire-sale buyer could receive possession without clean title. Build 66 reconnects that new failure mode to the much older Build 51 insurance system through the Interreality Title Indemnity Office.

A Build 63 fund can now buy title insurance on an owned claim or exchange bond before a Build 65 title dispute exists. The policy is attached to the asset rather than permanently to the original purchaser, so if the paper later changes hands the coverage follows the paper and any valid payout goes to the current Build 63 owner. Premiums come from fund cash and increase the selected Build 51 insurer's capital and reserves. Coverage, deductible, and premium are priced from the existence of a Build 64 repo, a Build 65 rehypothecation chain, payment injunctions, and the health of the first clearing-house lender.

Build 66 distinguishes ordinary title coverage from a rehypothecation rider. A standard policy written while a downstream reuse chain is already disclosed records that chain as an exclusion. If that exact disclosed chain later produces a title dispute, the insurer can deny the claim. The more expensive rehypothecation rider explicitly covers the known reuse risk. A standard policy written before an unexpected later reuse can still respond because the failed chain was not known when coverage was sold.

When a Build 65 title dispute appears against an insured asset, Build 66 automatically files a persistent title claim. A covered claim pays the current owner cash for the insured portion of the unresolved senior-title amount after the policy deductible. Crucially, the payout does not modify the Build 65 dispute, release the lien, or unfreeze the asset. The owner can therefore receive insurance money while still holding an `encumbered-title` or title-frozen asset. Build 66 treats cash indemnity and clean ownership as separate things.

Every paid title claim creates a subrogation claim. The insurer inherits a recovery right against the first lender that created the rehypothecation chain. Recovery is taken only from lender liquidity above an operating floor and is deliberately gradual: ordinary autonomous sweeps are capped at 0.55 per reconciliation, while a manual recovery action can press for up to 2.0. Recovered cash returns to the Build 51 insurer's capital. This means an insurer can pay a fund for a title loss and then drain clearing-house liquidity over time while the underlying title dispute remains unresolved.

The link to Build 51 is not cosmetic. Title payouts use the actual capital of existing Build 51 insurers. If a valid title claim cannot be fully funded, Build 66 records a coverage failure. If an insurer's capital falls below 3 after the payment, the insurer becomes `insolvent`, its active Build 51 software-risk policies become `orphaned`, and the old insurance layer can respond to that insolvency through its pre-existing public-reinsurance mechanics. A dispute about who owns a sovereign bond can therefore disable insurance on unrelated software contracts.

The main deterministic harness insured a 9.36 exchange bond during an active Build 65 rehypothecation using the enhanced rider. The policy carried about 6.7392 of coverage, a 0.404352 deductible, and a 0.643829 premium. When the same 3.7182 senior title loss used in the Build 65 liquidation path appeared, the insurer paid 3.313848 in cash. The bond remained title-frozen under `TDS1`. The resulting subrogation claim then recovered 0.55 from the first clearing house, reducing its liquidity from 12 to 11.45 and reducing the insurer's outstanding recovery claim from 3.313848 to 2.763848.

The exclusion harness bought a standard policy while a known rehypothecation chain was already active. When that same chain later failed, the claim was denied as `disclosed rehypothecation excluded by standard title policy` and paid zero. The failure harness instead insured an asset before any reuse existed, then introduced an unexpected rehypothecation and title loss after underwriting. With insurer capital deliberately reduced to 1.5, a valid claim could only pay 1.5, became `partial`, pushed the Build 51 insurer to `insolvent`, and changed an unrelated active Build 51 risk policy to `orphaned`.

Build 66 persists through `nothing-state-v66`, wraps the existing save/render chain, loads after `collateral_title.js`, and extends the forget action through version 66. It reads and selectively updates Build 51 insurer capital/status and software-risk policy status, Build 54 clearing-house liquidity, Build 62 claims and exchange bonds, Build 63 fund ownership/cash, Build 64 repo state, and Build 65 rehypothecations/title disputes. It does not rewrite the historical repo, rehypothecation, fire sale, or title-dispute records that created the insured loss.

New places include `insurance paid for ownership without delivering ownership`, `insurer inherited the right to be angry at a clearing house`, `cash compensation arrived but the lien stayed attached`, and `a sovereign title loss orphaned an unrelated software insurance policy`. `title_insurance.js` passes Node syntax validation, and the deterministic harness covers enhanced coverage, disclosed-reuse exclusion, partial insurer failure, Build 51 policy orphaning, and subrogation recovery against Build 54 liquidity.
