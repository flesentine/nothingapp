# Build 62

Debt became negotiable by vote. Build 61 created a remedies layer in which post-withdrawal external debt could survive as a recognized award, existing reserve stock could remain immune, and later reserve inflows could be garnished. Build 62 does not abolish those remedies. It creates a Sovereign Debt Restructuring Office above them and asks a different question: if an obligation is enforceable but the debtor still cannot sustainably carry it, who gets to decide what the obligation becomes next?

A reality with external debt can convene a persistent creditor conference. Opening the conference creates three claim blocks against the same debt: a 52% official creditor claim held by the other reality, a 25% interreality clearing-syndicate claim, and a 23% Archive distressed-claims position. If the debt is already represented by a recognized Build 61 surviving-debt award, the conference records that award and places its future-inflow lien under a creditor standstill. The award remains recognized, but execution pauses while restructuring negotiations are active. Build 62 can therefore create `enforceable judgment paused so creditors could negotiate its value` without pretending the judgment ceased to exist.

The debtor can draft either a moderate or deep exchange. The moderate exchange offers a 22% face-value haircut, a 5% coupon, and a 60-month replacement maturity. The deep exchange offers a 38% haircut, a 3.5% coupon, and an 84-month maturity. Creditors vote according to both the economic terms and the debtor's current debt-to-reserve distress. The office uses a 75% collective-action threshold. If enough claim weight accepts, the exchange becomes binding across the participating debt, including dissenters. External debt is written down, a new persistent exchange bond is created, the linked Build 61 award is resized to the restructured outstanding balance, and its execution lien resumes against the new amount rather than the old face value.

Collective action does not make minority creditors disappear. A dissenting claim that is dragged into a successful exchange becomes a bound holdout. A holdout can challenge the aggregation mechanism. When a formally sufficient vote is below 80%, Build 62 treats the aggregation as vulnerable: the holdout can win an injunction, escape the collective haircut, and restore the portion of old face value that the majority tried to erase from that claim. The exchange bond remains outstanding for participating creditors while the restored holdout claim sits beside it. The same historical debt can therefore exist partly as restructured paper and partly as an older promise revived by litigation.

Failed exchanges have their own consequences. If the collective-action threshold is not reached, the standstill ends and Build 61 enforcement resumes. A holdout can accelerate its claim, converting a portion of the failed restructuring into immediate trade-account arrears. The restructuring process can therefore improve solvency, fail harmlessly, or make the short-term payment problem worse depending on creditor coordination.

Build 62 persists through `nothing-state-v62` and keeps the save/render wrapper chain intact. It reads Build 57 trade-account external debt, Build 56 foreign reserves, and Build 61 recognized surviving-debt awards; it does not replace those layers. Successful exchanges update the shared external-debt balance and linked award amount because the obligation itself has legally changed. Standstills temporarily suppress the Build 61 lien and restore it when negotiations end. The final script is loaded after `remedies.js`, and the forget action now clears state through version 62.

Build 62 tracks the restructuring office, creditor conferences, creditor claims, exchange offers, collective-action votes, standstills, completed and failed restructurings, debt haircuts, replacement exchange bonds, bound dissenters, holdout challenges, injunctions, holdout victories, accelerated arrears, trust-audit history, incidents, and restructuring crises. New places can include `creditors became a government for one balance sheet`, `majority creditor vote changed the amount everyone was owed`, `old debt disappeared into a bond issued by the debtor who could not pay it`, `one creditor recovered the contract everyone else voted away`, `standstill ended before the debt changed`, and `minority creditor inherited the calendar`.

The Build 62 harness covers both major paths. With 12.0 of external debt and 4.0 of reserves, the moderate exchange received 77% support, cleared the 75% collective-action threshold, reduced debt to 9.36, and bound the 23% Archive holdout while restoring the Build 61 lien against the reduced balance. The holdout then won the deliberately weak aggregation challenge and restored 0.6072 of old face value, leaving 9.9672 of external debt. In a separate low-distress test, the deep 38% exchange received no support, failed, restored the paused Build 61 lien, and allowed the Archive holdout to accelerate 0.3312 into immediate arrears. `restructuring.js` also passes Node syntax validation.
