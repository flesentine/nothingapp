# Build 56

Settlement money acquired foreign exchange. Build 55 gave Reality A and Reality B separate Settlement Reserve Authorities capable of issuing RA and RB settlement reserves; Build 56 creates a persistent interreality FX market between those monies. The primary quotation is RA/RB, expressed as B-reserves per A-reserve. The market price is no longer decorative: it is pulled toward a fundamental value derived from the actual Build 55 balance sheets, including monetary-base expansion, bad assets, monetary-authority capital, discount rates, credibility, independence, and negative-equity status. Emergency monetary policy can therefore weaken or strengthen the currency that denominates the emergency settlement system.

Each reality now holds a finite stock of foreign reserves: Reality A holds RB and Reality B holds RA. A currency can float or declare a peg to the current interreality rate. Peg defense requires spending the other reality's reserves, not creating more of the domestic currency. This creates an intentional asymmetry with Build 55: a Settlement Reserve Authority can create unlimited amounts of its own settlement liability but cannot unilaterally print the foreign asset needed to defend its exchange-rate promise. Repeated intervention can exhaust the reserve stock, at which point the peg breaks while the domestic monetary authority can remain fully capable of issuing its own money.

Build 56 includes speculative attacks against misaligned pegs. A deteriorating Build 55 balance sheet can move the fundamental exchange rate away from the official peg; speculators then sell the pegged currency, market pressure moves the rate, and the monetary authority spends foreign reserves to defend its promise. Capital controls can reduce the effective size of the attack but weaken openness and do not repair the underlying monetary balance sheet. A peg can also be explicitly devalued, creating a persistent distinction between the same currency before and after an official change in its declared external value.

Foreign reserves can be accumulated and can become politically important in their own right. Reserve accumulation is not free: the purchasing monetary authority issues its own domestic settlement reserves to acquire the other reality's money, expanding the monetary base while improving its foreign-reserve stock. If one currency becomes a substantially larger share of the other reality's reserve holdings, the market can treat it as the interreality reserve currency. Build 56 also supports bilateral central-bank swap lines: one authority creates domestic reserves for the other while receiving reciprocally created foreign reserves in exchange. Swap lines can replenish the foreign-reserve stock needed for peg defense, but both monetary bases expand at the same time. The rescue of an exchange-rate promise can therefore create new monetary pressure that later changes the fundamental rate being defended.

The FX layer feeds its public institutional acts back into the existing trust history. FX-market creation, pegs, reserve-currency status, foreign-reserve accumulation, and central-bank swap lines can emit Build 40 trust-audit records, allowing Build 41 transparency and later consensus/execution to contain official statements about the monetary relationship between A and B. Currency crises open ordinary incidents for the observability and operations stack.

Build 56 tracks RA and RB currencies, the interreality exchange rate, monetary fundamentals, market pressure, turnover, floating and pegged regimes, peg bands, foreign-reserve stocks, interventions, reserve exhaustion, devaluation, capital controls, speculative attacks, reserve-currency status, reserve accumulation, bilateral reserve swap lines, trust-log integration, incidents, and FX crises. Crises can create places such as `foreign reserves defending a promise about money`, `exchange-rate promise outlived the reserves defending it`, `same currency new official value`, `market testing whether the peg has enough reserves behind it`, `foreign reserves created by reciprocal central-bank promise`, and `exchange rate translating one reality into another`, leaving the bailiff asking whether the currency is weak because people fled it or people fled because it was weak.
