# Build 55

Clearing acquired money. Build 54 centralized derivative settlement and exposed a public-liquidity bridge as the institution of last resort when a Software Clearing House froze. Build 55 makes that bridge explicit by creating a persistent Settlement Reserve Authority in Reality A and Reality B. Each authority has its own monetary base, lender-of-last-resort credit, discount rate, collateral floor, capital, bad-asset exposure, independence, credibility, emergency mandate, reserve accounts, balance-sheet audits, and the ability to continue operating even after the quality of its own assets deteriorates.

The core Build 55 distinction is liquidity versus solvency. Clearinghouses, Build 51 insurers, Build 47 package treasuries, and Build 53 derivatives dealers can be classified as solvent, illiquid, or insolvent from the state already created by earlier builds. Ordinary lender-of-last-resort credit is intended for institutions that are illiquid but still solvent and that can pledge eligible collateral. A user can deliberately override those rules and lend to an insolvent borrower or against collateral below the authority's current rating floor. The reserves are still created and the borrower can become operational again, but the balance-sheet hole does not disappear; the weak asset moves onto the monetary authority's balance sheet instead.

Emergency facilities create settlement reserves directly. The borrower receives spendable liquidity while the monetary authority's monetary base and outstanding credit expand. Facilities carry discount-window interest, maturity, a collateral type, face value, rating, haircut, lendable value, and a persistent record of whether the facility was undercollateralized or issued through a solvency override. Build 52 rating deterioration can therefore feed upward into Build 55: collateral can be revalued, a larger haircut can make an existing facility undercollateralized, and the monetary authority can issue its own collateral call. The borrower can meet that call by returning reserves, fail it, or receive explicit forbearance that leaves the emergency credit outstanding despite the collateral shortfall.

Build 55 integrates directly with Build 54 settlement finality. A failed or frozen clearinghouse can receive reserve credit and reopen without rewriting any prior settlement cycle. An emergency-final Build 54 cycle with an unpaid shortfall can later receive a monetary make-good. The new reserves pay the economic arrears, but the original cycle retains its historical shortfall and emergency-final status. The simulation can therefore contain a settlement that was procedurally final before it was economically paid, creating places such as `money arrived after settlement was declared final` and `finality came before payment`.

Emergency credit can also become permanent money. An active facility may be monetized: the monetary authority stops treating the exposure as repayable lender-of-last-resort credit and instead retains the collateral as a permanent public asset while the issued reserves remain in circulation. Repeated lending against weak collateral can make bad assets exceed the authority's own capital. The Settlement Reserve Authority then enters negative equity but continues issuing settlement money, producing the deliberate institutional paradox `central bank with negative equity still issuing reserves`. Discount rates and collateral floors are policy variables, so the authority can tighten or loosen the definition of acceptable emergency credit after the market has already adapted to the previous rule.

Build 55 emits monetary-authority creation, emergency facilities, collateral monetization, and settlement make-good records into the existing Build 40 trust-audit stream. That means Build 41 transparency and later quorum/state-machine layers can eventually treat monetary interventions as official institutional history. Monetary crises also open ordinary incidents for the existing observability and operations stack. Build 55 tracks monetary authorities, reserve accounts, monetary-base creation and extinguishment, lender-of-last-resort facilities, liquidity-versus-solvency classification, discount-window rates, collateral eligibility and rating floors, haircuts, undercollateralized lending, solvency overrides, collateral revaluation, central-bank collateral calls, forbearance, evergreen emergency facilities, collateral monetization, post-finality settlement make-goods, bad assets, negative-equity monetary authorities, trust-log integration, incidents, and monetary crises. Crises can create places such as `insolvent institution called illiquid by policy`, `liquidity created against collateral the rule rejected`, `settlement credits backed by emergency exception`, `central bank balance sheet holding software failure`, and `emergency loan became permanent money`, leaving the bailiff asking whether the money is good because the authority accepted the collateral or the collateral is good because the authority created the money.
